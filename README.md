# ReligieAtlas

A dependency-free web app for reading the Bible, Tanakh and Qur’an, and exploring biblical people and cities. Family maps offer three interactive SVG layouts: Constellation, Family tree, and Ancestry rings.

Live app: https://www.religieatlas.com/ · [Development handover](RESUME_BUILDING.md)

Vercel serves `dist/` through the existing `biblepath` project in `personalprojectsmichiel`. `vercel.json` disables installation/build commands and sets the static output directory. The GitHub production branch is `main`. The original private Sites publication remains available at https://biblepath-people-atlas.glossy-chord-2394.chatgpt.site/.

Dutch is the first-visit interface language; an explicitly saved English, Dutch or French preference is preserved. The Bible reader defaults to a complete Dutch Catholic collection based on the Petrus Canisius translation (1939), with explicitly sourced historical Catholic supplements where the digital transcription lacks text. The complete English Douay-Rheims 1899 edition and the existing 66-book World English Bible, Dutch Statenvertaling and French Louis Segond 1910 editions remain available. Interface language and text edition are distinct; saved edition preferences and reading positions are preserved. The language selector preserves reading and explorer state. Navigation starts with a religion: Christianity (Roman Catholic), Judaism or Islam. Each religion contains Holy books, People, Festivals & rituals, a Family map and a Cities explorer. Christianity retains its existing biblical atlas; Judaism and Islam use separate sourced family and place collections. Comparing traditions remains a shared entry. The selected religion stays consistent between books and people, while reading positions, editions and collection searches retain their own state. Testament filters apply only to the Bible index and family map.

The religion menu opens the appropriate Bible, Tanakh or Qur’an reader. The complete Tanakh follows Torah, Prophets and Writings: 24 canonical books represented by 39 navigation components, with Hebrew WLC and Jewish JPS 1917 editions. Dutch Statenvertaling and French Segond are explicitly labeled Christian translation aids. The complete Qur’an includes 114 surahs and 6,236 numbered ayahs, with Tanzil Arabic and named Dutch, English and French translations from QuranEnc. Hebrew and Arabic display right to left. Each edition retains its own numbering and saved reading position; equal verse numbers are not treated as alignment between Tanakh editions.

Tanakh and Qur’an readers support text/reference search, chapter or surah navigation, source credits and notes saved in this browser. Commentary is kept separate through external Sefaria and Quran.com links.

The People index has three separate collections: Bible identities from the original 2,987-person atlas and a Catholic supplement, 481 Torah identities drawn from first-five-book name associations (29 with curated Torah role profiles), and 60 curated Qur’an profiles. Torah and Qur’an IDs, searches and selected profiles remain separate; no family relationships are inferred between collections. The Across traditions view compares 24 selected figures in three sourced panels, with the Christian account explicitly following a Roman Catholic perspective and separately identified Catechism/Church-teaching references alongside biblical passages, distinguishing named, unnamed and outside-corpus accounts. Jewish panels may draw on the wider Tanakh; this does not expand the Torah index beyond its five-book scope.

The Dutch Catholic collection contains 73 books, 1,334 chapters and 35,510 verse records. The Canisius eBible export supplies the base, the original Canisius OSIS supplies the complete Daniel and Greek Esther additions, and Esther 15:1 plus 16:1–24 use J. M. van Oers (1933), separately attributed beside each of those 25 verses. It preserves historical Dutch spelling and is explicitly a composite reading edition. Psalm 84:9–10 is one text row with both locators. The [data notes](dist/data-notes.html#dutch-catholic) document source boundaries, rights and checksums.

The English Catholic reader contains all 73 books (46 Old Testament and 27 New Testament; [Catechism §120](https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_one/section_one/chapter_two/article_3/iv_the_canon_of_scripture.html)) in the public-domain [Douay-Rheims American Edition of 1899](https://ebible.org/details.php?id=engDRA): 1,334 chapters and 35,811 numbered verses. It includes Tobit, Judith, Wisdom, Sirach, Baruch, 1 and 2 Maccabees, the Greek additions to Esther (10:4–16:24) and Daniel (3:24–90; chapters 13–14), and the Letter of Jeremiah in Baruch 6. This edition retains Vulgate numbering. The Catholic people supplement supplies separate sourced profiles and passage references for this added corpus; it is not an exhaustive concordance or a new inferred genealogy. The original 2,987-person family graph and 604-city index retain their existing 66-book scope. WEB, Statenvertaling and Louis Segond remain unchanged 66-book editions, not complete Catholic translations. Jewish and Islamic profiles retain their distinct contexts and sources.

City references use the atlas's English verse numbering. When the selected edition has unaligned numbering for a book, the reader opens that citation in the English World English Bible with an explanation and a return control. The interface stays in the selected language; returning restores the previous reading position in that edition.

## Family maps and places by religion

Judaism and Islam have independent curated family maps and place explorers in `dist/tradition-atlas-judaism.json` and `dist/tradition-atlas-islam.json`. Every relationship cites its evidence. Parentage, spouses, siblings, care and adoption have distinct types; the Islamic collection distinguishes Qur’anic claims from later tradition. Jewish records follow the Tanakh. These are selective accounts, not exhaustive or independently established historical genealogies.

Choose a family, search/select people, zoom or scroll the SVG and inspect the relationship sources. Places offer search, type filtering, historical context, location uncertainty and sourced person associations. Maps, place selections and filters remain independent by religion. Reading links open edition-checked Hebrew Tanakh or Dutch Qur’an passages, with a return action. Optional person-index links only target existing identities. The existing Christian graph and its three layouts remain available.

Run `node tests/tradition-atlas-data.cjs`, `node tests/tradition-atlas-ui.cjs` and `node tests/interfaith-app.cjs` to verify source/schema boundaries, real scripture coordinates, graph geometry, interactions, language changes, asynchronous loading and return navigation.

## Festivals and rituals

Each religion has its own **Feestdagen & rituelen** submenu as an information source: seven Roman Catholic dossiers, eight Jewish dossiers and seven Islamic dossiers. The collection covers celebrations, seasons and recurrent rituals rather than an exhaustive calendar. Each dossier supplies a recurring calendar rule, meaning and texts, historical context, practice, internal diversity, attributed references in Dutch, English and French. Sacred texts, tradition/interpretation and historical studies have distinct labels; numbered citations connect claims to their sources.

The editorial assets are `dist/festivals-christianity.json`, `dist/festivals-judaism.json` and `dist/festivals-islam.json`. They load on demand and keep religion-specific search, filtering and selection state. Calendar rules are descriptive, not automatically calculated annual dates. Institutional religious sources represent their stated perspectives, not a consensus of all adherents; historical accounts and theological claims must remain distinguishable.

Internal reading links carry reviewed edition-specific coordinates: Dutch Catholic `catholic-nl`, Hebrew Tanakh `he`, and Dutch Qur’an `nl`. The Jewish links deliberately use Hebrew numbering rather than assuming the Dutch Christian reading aid shares it. Original sources remain available beside the local reader action. A return control restores the selected information dossier. Tanakh and Qur’an references now retain and highlight full verse ranges. Scripture wording and imported source assets are unchanged.

Run `node tests/festivals-data.cjs` for source IDs, languages, distinct religious scope and every internal passage against the actual editions; `node tests/festivals-ui.cjs` for searches, filters, source actions and per-religion state; and `node tests/interfaith-app.cjs` for asynchronous navigation, retries, language changes and reader return paths. Source interpretation still requires editorial review, beyond automated schema and coordinate checks.

## Run locally

From this directory, run `python -m http.server 5173 --directory dist` and open http://localhost:5173.

There is no build step. `dist/` contains the editable source and deployable app. The original base atlas contains 2,987 distinct people, 1,803 family relationships, and over 20,000 reference links across the 66 shared books. It combines the 157 original curated profiles with a pinned STEP Bible TIPNR snapshot under CC BY 4.0. Catholic supplemental profiles add coverage without changing that source graph. This is broad coverage, not a claim of an exhaustive or universally agreed genealogy.

## Interactions

- Choose two people and find their connecting path across the entire collection. Bible-cited links are the default; indexed links can be included explicitly. Directed ancestry takes priority, then shortest family paths. Gold path highlights persist across zoom, selection, and layouts. Every step retains its source and notes. Adam → Jesus has 63 connections and includes legal parenthood.
- Read the complete Dutch Catholic collection or English Douay-Rheims 1899, including all seven deuterocanonical books and the Esther/Daniel additions. Search and navigate by book, chapter or reference. Each edition has its own saved position, numbering, source credits and additions shortcuts.
- Read the existing public-domain World English Bible edition: 66 books, 1,189 chapters, 31,103 numbered verses, or its Dutch/French reading alternatives. Follow annotated names to family maps or cities. Exact-verse identities take priority only where numbering is aligned; uncertain matches and namesakes use a chooser. Psalm headings and source footnotes are retained where present in the source.
- Switch traditions and text editions without losing their separate reading positions. Search the Tanakh or Qur’an, read original scripts and named translations, and save personal reflections by edition and reading. Notes remain local to the current browser; commentary links open the source website.
- Select Bible, Torah or Qur’an in the people index. Search localized names, aliases, roles and passages; open a profile and follow its sources. The sidebar quick search and family map remain explicitly Bible-scoped.
- Open Across traditions to explore each figure’s role in Roman Catholic, Jewish and Islamic accounts, distinguishing biblical passages from Catholic doctrine. Comparison links open the corresponding collection profile; unnamed figures and differences in textual scope are labeled.
- Browse 604 cities and towns with 4,125 verse references. Search/filter the index, open sourced place profiles, read their passages, and explore people indexed in the same verses. These shared passages do not imply residence or physical presence.
- Switch among three layouts over the same data.
- Adaptive zoom starts with immediate family. Zooming out reveals wider branches (up to eight family steps); zooming in near a visible person reveals their immediate relatives. Already revealed branches remain until reset or a new family is chosen.
- Scene expansion preserves existing node positions and the camera. If a newly revealed family-tree chain cannot fit between existing generations, the tree rearranges around the same anchor to keep parents above children. Circles have at least 126 world units between centers, and obstacle-aware routes avoid unrelated circles. Close zoom adds selective relationship labels.
- Pan, pinch, wheel zoom, keyboard pan/zoom, fit, and expanded map.
- Hover or focus a person for a biography and source; select to inspect their family.
- Search by names, aliases, summaries, and references; browse the people index.
- Filter by testament or choose a fixed relationship distance. Large scenes show up to 180 people with an explicit remaining count; all people remain searchable in the paginated index.
- Read notes for legal parenthood and differing genealogical accounts.

## Extending the index

The repeatable people import is `node scripts/import-tipnr.cjs`. It downloads a pinned source into ignored `.cache/`, applies explicit identity mappings, source corrections, and relationship exclusions, then regenerates `dist/data.js` and the import report. Source identity suffixes are case-sensitive. Do not lowercase them or merge namesakes by display name. `python scripts/import-bible.py` imports the official eBible.org WEB USFX snapshot, and `node scripts/import-places.cjs` creates the settlement index from pinned STEP and OpenBible data. Download checksums and attribution are stored with the datasets. Bible, Tanakh, Qur’an and city JSON files load only when needed.

Run `python scripts/import-tanakh.py` and `python scripts/import-quran.py` to rebuild the additional readers. Tanakh’s Dutch/French aids reuse the existing Bible edition assets; regenerate those first if their sources change. Both new importers accept `--check` to reproduce and compare the saved asset without rewriting it. Preserve exact source wording and all attribution: Hebrew WLC and JPS 1917 are public domain; Tanzil requires verbatim Arabic text, its copyright notice and source link; QuranEnc translations retain publisher, version, transcript information and republication terms. Before publishing a new release, review each Qur’an edition’s `checkUpdatesUrl`, then review any changed source before updating checksum pins. A cached reproducibility check does not check for upstream updates.

Run `python scripts/import-catholic-dutch.py` to reproduce `dist/catholic-bible-data-nl.json`, or add `--check` for verification. The Dutch collection has the fixed edition ID `catholic-nl`, independent of interface language. Its metadata records every source, checksum, source-specific supplement and processing rule. The importer never translates or invents missing scripture. Keep all passage source notes and independently verify the Greek additions; a count of 73 books alone does not establish completeness.

Run `python scripts/import-catholic-bible.py` to reproduce `dist/catholic-bible-data.json`, or add `--check` to verify the saved asset. Its official eBible USFX download is checksum-pinned; the importer preserves all 73 books, wording, punctuation and source verse numbers with documented whitespace normalization. Do not reuse legacy WEB/STEP verse coordinates as exact Douay-Rheims identities: its Vulgate numbering is independent. Catholic supplemental citations explicitly name the edition and link to its source passages.

Catholic person citations retain the fixed English `catholic` edition ID. The person index’s primary reading action opens the reviewed Dutch Catholic counterpart. A separate original-source link retains the cited Douay-Rheims passage. The reader displays both source and target coordinates, highlights full ranges, and offers separate targets when a source passage crosses chapters or changes order. Both Catholic editions offer the same supplemental people collection, while exact-verse annotations remain restricted to their reviewed source edition.

Person passage navigation uses `dist/person-passage-map.js` and lazily loads `dist/person-passage-map.json`. Rebuild with `node scripts/build-person-passage-map.cjs`; use `--check` for reproducibility. Source labels and edition IDs remain immutable. The resolver accepts only reviewed entries and rejects unknown source editions or unmapped labels instead of guessing by matching numbers. Partial correspondences and textual variants have visible explanations. This navigation mapping does not change atlas identity evidence, city references, or Torah/Qur’an sources. See [mapping provenance and coverage](docs/person-passage-mapping.md) for the STEP TVTMS attribution, reviewed exceptions and numbering examples.

Catholic supplemental profiles are authored in `scripts/catholic-people-copy.cjs` and built into `dist/catholic-people.json` by `node scripts/build-catholic-people.cjs`; use `--check` for deterministic verification. They retain namespaced identities, NL/EN/FR summaries, explicit mappings to an existing atlas identity where reviewed, and source-edition passage coordinates. They add no inferred graph edges.

Every person has `name`, `gender`, `testament`, `era`, `description`, `refs`, and identity/source context. Relationships use `source`, `target`, `type` (`parent` or `spouse`), `ref`, `evidence`, and an optional `note`. Parent arrows point from parent to child. Do not silently merge different genealogical accounts or infer missing biological relatives.

The separate collection assets are `torah-people.json`, `quran-people.json` and `person-comparisons.json`. Their names, roles, summaries and scope notes are authored in NL/EN/FR; IDs are namespaced and comparison links are explicit. `node scripts/build-torah-people.cjs --check` and `node scripts/build-quran-people.cjs --check` verify deterministic regeneration. Torah index links retain the original English ESV/KJV locators, while curated Torah roles cite Jewish JPS 1917. Links do not assume that Hebrew or other edition numbering is identical. Qur’an profiles cite actual passages; later traditional names are not substituted for unnamed people.

Imported relationships cite the exact STEP index record rather than an arbitrary Bible verse. Name references are labeled as indexed and can include descendant/group usages of an ancestral name. Original curated references retain KJV links; imported name references use ESV. New descriptions use structured identity and role fields, not the source's AI-generated biographies. Full attribution and editorial changes appear in `dist/data-notes.html`.

## Validation

Person-reference checks include `node tests/person-passage-map.cjs`, `node tests/person-passage-resolver.cjs` and `node scripts/build-person-passage-map.cjs --check`, together with app and reader integration tests. The full validation list is in [the handover](RESUME_BUILDING.md#validate-before-publishing). Dutch Catholic checks are `node tests/catholic-dutch-data.cjs`, `python tests/import-catholic-dutch.py` and `python scripts/import-catholic-dutch.py --check`. English Catholic scripture checks are `node tests/catholic-bible-data.cjs`, `python tests/import-catholic-bible.py` and `python scripts/import-catholic-bible.py --check`; they verify the canon, all added passages, source integrity and every imported verse against the original XML. People checks include `node tests/torah-people.cjs`, `node tests/quran-people.cjs`, `node tests/person-comparisons.cjs` and `node tests/people-traditions.cjs`. The app integration suite also verifies separate index navigation, asynchronous loading and comparison links. Also run the scripture and people generator `--check` commands for reproducibility.

Checks cover source provenance, complete scripture editions, native-script search, numbering boundaries, saved positions/notes, language and tradition switching, late or failed downloads, namesakes, paths, persistent highlights, progressive discovery, parent/child generation order, circle/arrow clearance and edition-safe city references. The place rebuild check requires the pinned `.cache/TIPNR.txt` and `.cache/OpenBible-ancient.jsonl` inputs used by `scripts/import-places.cjs`.

Google Fonts provides typography with system fallbacks. The application needs no external runtime library or data service after its static assets load.
